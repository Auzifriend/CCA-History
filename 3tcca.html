CCA History

The Carry Cancel Adder (CCA) is an adder circuit used in Computational Redstone which uses generate-kill signals instead of generate-propogate. It was created over a decade ago and has cemented itself as the most used adder in computational redstone because of its speed, size, and synchronous output. But who made it? why is it so good? And what are the current inovations? This page will answer all of those questions and more.

The CCA was created by a user named 'MagicalGentleman', or simply just 'Magic', although now known as 'moppu' in September 2014. It was orignally 5 redstone ticks (5t) and 6x11 blocks long. here is Magics explanation of the adder:

The adder is comprised of 3 stages:

In stage 1, the adder performs XOR, AND, and NOR. XOR is pretty straightforward. It's just the sum without the carry. AND is the Generate signal G. NOR is the Carry Cancel signal CC.

In stage 2, the G and CC signals are sent to two separate slab stacks. The G signal goes to a slab stack that connects to the base input of every comparator in the CCA carry tower. The CC signal goes to a slab stack that connects to the side input of every carry tower comparator.

When these signals hit the slab stacks, you need to provide full power (15 signal strength). The carry calculation works in ratios of signal strength so make sure that your input strengths are all the same! Also keep in mind that these slab stacks are supposed to 'bleed upwards' to the other comparators, that's the core of how this system works.

The two slab stacks are used by a comparator at each level to calculate whether there is a valid carry. The G signal is what is passed through the comparator, and the CC signal is what goes into the side for comparison. If the signal strength read from the CC stack is higher than the signal strength from G, the carry is 'cancelled'.

This works because a physically higher CC will always have a higher signal strength than a G signal from below. The same goes for if G is stronger than CC, it can only mean that G happened at least 1 level higher than CC. This would force signal out of the comparator as the CC signal can't subtract it all. This comparator's output would signify that a carry happened, and needs to be XORed in stage 3.

In stage 3, the output of the comparators of stage 2 are fed into a final XOR with the first XOR result from stage 1. The output of this XOR is the final result.

In summary:
Stage 1: xor, and, nor. (2 ticks)
Stage 2: carry calculations. (1 tick)
Stage 3: final xor. (2 ticks) (This can be 1 tick with a special type of XOR I designed)
This adder was also, incredibly, made independently by a user named 'yap7' a few months after Magics first creation. Whilst he wasnt the first creator, as Magic also says, he should have just as much credit for its creation.

The CCA was revolutionary because its carry calculation logic was much smaller that other adders at the time such as CLE or CLA and it was one of the first actualy good vertical adders which made vertical redstone much more feasible. However the CCA would not catch on just yet - The adder stood at 5t while the PLA and CLEs of this time had reached speeds of 3t, making the CCA currently not good enough to use competitively. However, over the next few years, the CCA would overcome all boundries and become the most widely used adder today.


Magic's CCA

Some new Optimisations:

  • In late January of 2015, Magic made the 5t CCA 4-wide, and found out he could use XNOR for generate instead of AND which made the adder simpler.
  • At the end of January, he made the adder 4t using a special XOR although having low Signal Strength (SS) out
  • In February, Magic and 'Embizone' created a 4-wide, 4t CCA with low SS output
  • In late February/ Early March - Magic created a 5-wide 4t CCA with min 4SS output


By August 2015, the idea of a 3t CCA starts popping up, First from Yap7 who suggests a faux 4t ALU using a 4t CCA by reading both A and B from registers as well as !A and !B, then muxing between these which gives the perception of 1t inverters and a 3t CCA although it uses a 4t CCA. In January 2016, 'LambdaPi' noticed that the main issue with the CCA was that XNOR takes 2t to calculate. inverting one of the inputs will turn the XNOR into XOR which can be calculated in 1t, the NOR then becomes NIMPLIES which can also be calculated in 1t. This adder with inverted input was 3t, however because of SS, it was only 4 bits. In July 2016, Embizone created a 3t CCA with inverted output (they gave no more information on forums) In February 2016, 'CrazyPyroEagle' made a 3t CCA using comparator priming In August 2017, 'Zijkhal' made a 2t CCA using comparator priming with Koyarnos/alex_you/Nikkeru_ 3t CCA.
